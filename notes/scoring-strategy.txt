Where this will break (predictably)
1. top_k = 10 is too small long-term

With:

~140 chunks per book

multiple books on similar themes

You’ll often see:

1 chunk from many books

no book accumulating enough evidence

This makes results unstable.

Symptom: different queries return different “top books” for similar memories.

2. Pure counts ignore strength of match

Right now:

a weak similarity match counts the same as a strong one

This causes:

noise chunks to dominate

long books to win by chance

3. One strong chunk ≠ recall confidence

A single very strong chunk should not beat:

4–5 good matches from another book

Your intuition already points beyond this.

Minimal next improvements (still simple)
1. Increase retrieval depth

Change:

top_k = 10


to:

top_k = 30 or 50


This gives books room to accumulate evidence.

2. Use similarity scores, not just counts

Instead of:

book_hits[book_id] += 1


Use:

book_score[book_id] += similarity_score


Counts can be kept as a secondary signal.

3. Cap contribution per chunk

Prevent a single chunk from dominating:

chunk_contribution = min(similarity_score, 0.85)

A better still-simple scoring formula

For each retrieved chunk:

book_score[book_id] += similarity_score
book_hits[book_id] += 1


Final score:

final_score = book_score * log(1 + book_hits)


This:

rewards multiple matches

still respects strength

avoids long-book bias

No normalization needed yet.
